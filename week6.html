Week 6 – Reflection on Learning Outcomes

Algorithmic identity refers to how algorithms use our online data, behaviours and activities to classify and define who we are. Rather than simply reflecting our real identities, platforms construct identities based on patterns such as browsing history, likes and searches. In my experience, social media recommendations and targeted advertisements often reflect my interests based on my online activities. This shows that algorithms can shape how platforms perceive me, sometimes without fully understanding who I really am.

Sumpter uses different characteristics and patterns of behaviour to classify his friends, organising them into categories based on their interests, lifestyles and social relationships. His framework shows how algorithms can simplify complex human identities into measurable data. However, I think some categories are missing, such as cultural background, personal values and emotional connections. People are complex and constantly changing, so they cannot be fully represented by fixed categories or numerical data.

I collected the content data posted by my friends on the Weibo platform. As an open platform accessible to everyone, not just friends, I noticed that my friends were more inclined to post content related to lifestyle, animals, etc., while they posted less about private matters such as family and partners. This indicates that each user's "identity" presented on different platforms may vary.
